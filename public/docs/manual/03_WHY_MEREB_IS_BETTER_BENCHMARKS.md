## 1. Executive Summary

Game developers building web, mobile, and indie multiplayer games face a recurring economic crisis: **Dedicated multiplayer server costs often exceed the total ad and in-app purchase revenue generated by the game.**

On platforms like **Poki**, **CrazyGames**, **Itch.io**, and mobile web, games generate an average effective eCPM between **\$1.50 and \$4.00 per 1,000 game impressions**. Running dedicated game containers on platforms like **Hathora**, **Agones**, or **AWS GameLift** costs between **\$0.03 and \$0.08 per server-hour**. If a game has 1,000 Concurrent Users (CCU) across 500 active matches, running those servers costs **\$350 to \$1,200 per month** in pure compute and bandwidth egress fees—eradicating profitability.

**Mereb re-engineers this paradigm from the ground up.** By running authoritative 60Hz gameplay over direct **WebRTC Peer-to-Peer DataChannels** and offloading matchmaking to serverless edge workers (**Cloudflare Workers / PartyKit**), **infrastructure costs drop to \$0/month for up to 10,000 CCU.**

## 3. Deep-Dive: 6 Critical Reasons Mereb Outperforms the Competition

### 1. Eliminating the "Bandwidth Egress Trap"
Dedicated server platforms (Hathora, AWS GameLift, Agones) act as centralized relays: all 60Hz physics telemetry from all players flows into a cloud datacenter and is mirrored back down. At 60Hz with 1,000 CCU, a typical game streams **250 to 500 Gigabytes of bandwidth every single day**. Cloud providers charge up to **\$0.09 per Gigabyte** in egress fees.
* **With Mereb**: 60Hz state travels **directly peer-to-peer** between player devices over WebRTC DataChannels. The edge signaling server only handles lightweight JSON room handshakes (<2 KB per match). Egress costs remain essentially **zero**.

### 2. Zero-Cold-Start Matchmaking (<10ms vs 45 Seconds)
Platforms like Hathora, Agones, and GameLift spin up on-demand Docker containers for each game instance. When a sudden traffic spike occurs (e.g. a popular streamer plays your game, or Poki features it on their homepage), container provisioning causes **queue wait times of 30 to 90 seconds**.
* In web gaming, **70% of players bounce if a match does not start within 8 seconds.**
* **With Mereb**: Rooms are created instantly in Cloudflare Durable Objects (<10ms globally). No containers to pull, no VMs to spin up, no cold boots.

### 3. Built-In Poki & CrazyGames Video Ad Freeze Resilience
When a player watches a rewarded video ad or triggers a mid-roll interstitial on Poki or CrazyGames, web browsers aggressively throttle background JavaScript execution:
* Traditional WebSocket connections to Photon or dedicated servers time out and disconnect due to missing ping/pong heartbeats. The player returns from the ad to find their connection dropped and match lost.
* **With Mereb**: The built-in `PortalManager` hooks the portal lifecycle (`ad-start`, `ad-end`). It automatically pauses the simulation tick, silences network timeout assertions, and smoothly resynchronizes entity positions upon ad completion.

### 4. Smart Bot Fallback (Eradicating Empty Lobbies)
One of the fastest ways for an indie game to die is the "empty queue death spiral": player A queues for matchmaking, waits 20 seconds, leaves. Player B queues 10 seconds later, finds no one, and also leaves.
* **With Mereb**: If a peer is not matched within `botTimeoutMs` (default: 8,000ms), the client instantly promotes itself to local host and activates a smart bot. The player immediately experiences real gameplay without knowing the lobby was momentarily quiet.

### 5. Ultra-Compact Binary Serialization (85% Bandwidth Reduction)
Most web games transmit stringified JSON snapshots (`{"x": 124.523, "y": 89.212, "health": 100, ...}`) over WebSockets, consuming 300–800 bytes per tick and generating heavy Garbage Collector (GC) allocation pauses.
* **With Mereb**: `defineSchema` compiles state definitions down to contiguous binary byte arrays (30–45 bytes total) utilizing 16-bit fixed-point precision. This eliminates GC stutter and delivers buttery 120 FPS rendering.

### 6. Sub-15ms Direct Physical Latency
Centralized game servers are hosted in fixed cloud regions (e.g., `us-east-1` in Virginia). If two players in Germany or Japan play a 1v1 match, their traffic must travel thousands of miles to the US and back, resulting in a minimum latency of 80–140ms.
* **With Mereb**: The two players establish a direct WebRTC peer socket. Traffic travels straight across their local ISPs, achieving physical latencies as low as **5ms to 18ms**.
